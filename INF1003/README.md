# INF1003 Mathematics 1

INF1003 introduces discrete mathematics through three conceptual blocks:
mathematical foundations (sequences, number theory, combinatorics), logic and
proof (propositional and predicate logic, rules of inference, proof methods),
and discrete structures (sets, functions, relations). The module follows Kenneth
H. Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications*, 8th Edition.

These materials cover the AY25/26 Trimester 1 run of the module. Assessment
formats and schedules may differ in another run.

## Learning Focus

By working through the module material, students develop the ability to:

- manipulate sequences, summations, and products; apply arithmetic and geometric
  progressions;
- reason about divisibility, modular arithmetic, primes, GCD, LCM, and the
  Euclidean algorithm;
- count using product and sum rules, permutations, combinations, subtraction
  and division rules, and the pigeonhole principle;
- translate between natural language and propositional or predicate logic;
- apply logical equivalences, De Morgan's laws, and rules of inference;
- construct direct, contrapositive, contradiction, exhaustive, and
  existence/uniqueness proofs;
- work with set notation, operations, identities, cardinality, power sets,
  and Cartesian products;
- classify functions as injective, surjective, or bijective; compose and invert
  functions; and prove function properties;
- analyse relations for reflexivity, symmetry, antisymmetry, and transitivity;
  and identify equivalence relations.
