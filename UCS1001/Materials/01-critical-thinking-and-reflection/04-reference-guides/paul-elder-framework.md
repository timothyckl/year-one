# Paul-Elder Framework Reference

Quick-reference sheet for analysing and evaluating reasoning. The framework
improves the quality of thinking by taking charge of the structures inherent in
thinking and imposing intellectual standards upon them.

## The Three Components

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Elements of Thought | The eight structures present in all reasoning |
| Intellectual Standards | Criteria used to judge the quality of reasoning |
| Intellectual Traits | Habits cultivated by routinely applying the standards |

## Elements Of Thought

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Point of view | The "place" from which you view something; what you are looking at and how you are seeing it |
| Purpose | Your goal, objective, or what you are trying to accomplish |
| Question at issue | The problem or issue that lays out and guides the thinking |
| Information | Facts, data, evidence, or experiences used to figure things out |
| Inferences | Interpretations or conclusions you come to from the evidence |
| Concepts | Ideas, theories, laws, principles, or hypotheses used to make sense of things |
| Assumptions | Beliefs taken for granted, usually operating subconsciously |
| Implications and consequences | What logically follows from thoughts (implications) and from actions (consequences) |

**Mnemonic:** think for a purpose, within a point of view, based on assumptions,
leading to implications and consequences, using data, facts, and experiences, to
make inferences and interpretations, based on concepts and theories, to address
a question, issue, or problem.

## Checking Questions Per Element

| Element | Example checking questions |
|---|---|
| Point of view | Is my view the only reasonable view? What does my point of view ignore? Have I considered other relevant viewpoints? |
| Purpose | What is the purpose in doing this? Is the purpose clear and justifiable? |
| Question at issue | What is the question I am trying to answer? Is there a better way to put it? What kind of question is this? |
| Information | What information do I need? Is it accurate and relevant? Have we left out important information? |
| Inferences | Is my inference logical? Does our solution follow from our data? Is there an alternative plausible conclusion? |
| Concepts | What idea am I using? Is the concept being used accurately? |
| Assumptions | What am I taking for granted? Am I assuming something I should not? |
| Implications | If I decide to do X, what might happen? What are you implying when you say that? |

## Intellectual Standards

| Standard | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clarity | The idea can be understood |
| Accuracy | The information is true and free from error |
| Precision | The idea is specific and exact |
| Relevance | The information connects to the question at issue |
| Depth | The reasoning addresses the complexity of the issue |
| Breadth | Alternative points of view are considered |
| Logic | The reasoning makes sense and follows on |
| Significance | The focus is on what matters most |
| Sufficiency (completeness) | There is enough information and nothing necessary is missing |
| Fairness | The reasoning is free from bias and self-interest |

## Criteria For Evaluating Reasoning

| Element of thought | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Clearly stated or implied? Significant? |
| Question at issue | Well-stated? Clear and unbiased? Does justice to the complexity? Relevant to the purpose? |
| Information | Relevant evidence, experiences, or information? Accurate? Addresses complexities? |
| Inferences and interpretations | A line of reasoning explaining how conclusions are reached? |
| Concepts | Key concepts clarified? Used accurately? |
| Assumptions | Sensitivity to what is taken for granted? Questionable assumptions addressed? |
| Implications and consequences | Sensitivity to the implications and consequences of the position? |
| Points of view | Alternative relevant points of view considered? Objections answered? |

## Intellectual Traits

| Trait | Reflective question |
|---|---|
| Intellectual humility | What do I really know about this issue? Do my prejudices bias my judgment? |
| Intellectual courage | Am I willing to yield my position when evidence is against it? Stand against the majority? |
| Intellectual empathy | Do I accurately represent viewpoints with which I disagree? |
| Intellectual integrity | Do I expect of myself what I expect of others? Do I eliminate self-deception? |
| Intellectual perseverance | Am I willing to work through complexities instead of giving up? |
| Confidence in reason | Do I change my position when the evidence leads to a more reasonable one? |
| Intellectual autonomy | Do I think issues through myself rather than accept others' conclusions? |
| Fairmindedness | Am I giving dissenting opinions adequate consideration? Has bias clouded my judgment? |
