# Week 1 - Systems Thinking and Feedback Loops

## Overview

Beyond the elements of thought, the module encourages you to think about how
things are connected. Three concepts are central to this way of thinking:
**interconnection**, the **big picture**, and **feedback loops**. These apply to
analysis, evaluation, and decision-making, and they work alongside the attention
to details found in the Paul-Elder framework.

## Interconnection

Systems are made of parts that affect one another. A change in one part ripples
through the others.

Example: public transportation in a city. Buses, trains, roads, traffic,
pollution, and citizens' choices all affect one another. If more people take the
bus, there are fewer cars on the road, which means less traffic and cleaner air.

## Big Picture

Good decisions are rarely about a single metric. A system should be understood
in its wider context.

Example: good transportation is not just about buses being on time. It is
connected to economic growth, the environment, people's health, and access to
jobs and schools.

## Feedback Loops

A feedback loop is a cycle in which an outcome influences the behaviour that
produced it. A reinforcing loop amplifies change, while a balancing loop
counteracts change and moves the system towards stability.

| Loop type | Pattern | Public transport example |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforcing (virtuous cycle) | Success builds on success | Buses are reliable and affordable -> more people use them -> the city invests more in the system -> service improves -> even more people switch to public transport. |
| Reinforcing (vicious cycle) | Decline feeds decline | Unreliable service -> fewer riders -> less funding -> worse service -> even fewer riders. |

Both transport examples reinforce their initial direction, even though one has
desirable results and the other has undesirable results. A balancing loop would
instead oppose the initial change.

## Using These Concepts

You can use interconnection, big picture, and feedback loops both when
critiquing others' ideas and when developing your own.

When critiquing someone else's work, ask:

- Has the author considered interconnection?
- Has the author considered the big picture?
- Has the author considered feedback loops?

When shaping your own ideas, reflect by asking:

- Am I seeing connections?
- Am I thinking big picture?
- What feedback loops might this create?

## Practice

Pick a system you know (for example, an online platform, a campus service, or a
daily routine) and answer:

1. Which parts are interconnected, and how does a change in one affect the
   others?
2. What is the big picture beyond the immediate outcome?
3. What positive and negative feedback loops might exist?
4. Would your analysis be complete if you ignored any one of these three
   concepts?
