# Week 3: Reader Response Practice

Practice tasks to consolidate the reader response unit. Work through them in
order, alone or with a partner, before planning your own response.

## Task 1: Reader response essentials (peer teaching prep)

Before class, prepare to explain the reader response essay to your peers. Be
ready to share your understanding and to receive peer and teacher feedback.
Research and note down the key points on:

- What a reader response essay is and what it is for.
- The structure of a reader response essay (introduction, body, conclusion).
- The components of each part (thesis, analysis, evidence, conclusion).

Tip: you can use a critical dialogue with an AI tool (e.g. ChatGPT) to check
your understanding, but always verify its claims against the notes in the
reader response lecture and the structure reference guide.

## Task 2: Assess the claims in a sample reader response

Read the sample reader response below, then evaluate it in terms of two points:

1. How well it presents the student writer's thesis and supports it.
2. How well it refers to/cites information in the original article - including
   the reported speech/language used to introduce the article and the verb
   tenses used in those verbs.

### Sample reader response

The use of bio-based and recycled composites in aircraft structures can reduce
environmental impacts, such as greenhouse gas emissions (Brothers, 2021).
Lightweight synthetic composites are commonly used in aircraft structures due
to their exceptional mechanical properties, which reduces fuel consumption and
emissions (Brothers, 2021). Bio-based composites are recommended for use in
secondary structures and interior composites of aircraft, which are not
subjected to much stress compared to primary structures (Brothers, 2021).
However, the lack of confidence in their mechanical properties limits their
application in primary structures. Life Cycle Assessment is a tool used to
identify potential bio-based and recycled materials that can be used in
aircraft, assessing the environmental impacts of materials (Brothers, 2021).

Carbon fiber is a commonly used composite in aircraft structures due to its
high mechanical properties, and its production methods and mechanical
properties are discussed in the article (Brothers, 2021). Bio-based fibers,
such as Flax and Ramie, have been researched as more eco-friendly alternatives
to synthetic fibers, such as Carbon HS (Brothers, 2021). However, they have
downsides such as poor tensile and bending properties and higher energy
requirements during manufacturing (Brothers, 2021). Bio-based resins, such as
Furan and bio-based epoxy resins, can also be used to protect the environment
(Brothers, 2021).

Recycling carbon fibers, instead of incinerating them in landfills, is another
way to reduce emissions (Michael Abrams, 2021). Pyrolysis is the process best
suited for recycling carbon fibers, as it decomposes the organic part of carbon
fibers at temperatures ranging from 450 degrees Celsius to 700 degrees Celsius
(Fernandez et al., 2018). However, pyrolysis deteriorates the carbon fiber
after recycling. A new 2-part process, consisting of pyrolysis followed by
oxidation, helps retain the mechanical and physical properties of recycled
carbon fiber (Michael Abrams, 2021).

Although bio composites have environmental advantages and low costs, their
mechanical properties cannot fulfill the requirements of primary aircraft
structures at present (Brothers, 2021). However, they can be used in secondary
structures and interior composites (Brothers, 2021). Kuram (2021) discusses
that bio composites made from natural fibers are economical, which could
significantly reduce the total costs of aircraft manufacturing. The recycling
of carbon fibers can reduce emissions by 90% to 95% (Michael Abrams, 2021),
and a new recycling process consisting of pyrolysis followed by oxidation can
retain the mechanical and physical properties of recycled carbon fiber
(Michael Abrams, 2021).

In conclusion, the implementation of bio composites in aircraft structures is
limited by their mechanical properties, but they can be used in secondary
structures and interior composites. Recycling carbon fibers can significantly
reduce emissions, and the use of a new recycling process can retain the
mechanical and physical properties of recycled carbon fiber. Together, these
measures can help reduce the carbon footprint of aircraft.

### Evaluation questions

- Where is the student writer's thesis or position statement? Is it clear and
  consistently supported?
- Which parts present the article's content, and which parts offer the
  student's own analysis?
- How are sources introduced? What reporting verbs and verb tenses are used?
- Are the citations formatted consistently? Can you spot any weaknesses in the
  referencing (e.g. inconsistent use of author names)?

## Task 3: Paul-Elder framework review

With a partner, review the sample essay using the Paul-Elder critical thinking
framework as your point of reference. Answer this question: how does the text
fare in terms of the ten standards of thinking (clarity, accuracy, relevance,
logic, breadth, precision, significance, completeness, fairness, depth)?

Then examine the text against the elements of thought:

- Purpose (of the reader response).
- Point of view (the student writer's thesis/main claim).
- Assumptions of the student writer's thinking.
- Implications and consequences (of agreeing with the opinion).
- Data, facts, and experiences (evidence offered).
- Concepts and theories (what concepts/theories need to be understood).
- Questions, issues, and problems (brought forward in the article).

## Task 4: Plan your own reader response

Using the critical response template, plan a reader response to one takeaway
from your assigned article.

1. **Introduction:** note the title, author, and date of publication; briefly
   outline the author's thesis or claim; draft your own thesis statement about
   the text.
2. **Body:** identify the takeaway you will respond to and your perspective;
   list the evidence and credible sources you will use; note any
   counterarguments you should acknowledge.
3. **Conclusion:** plan to restate your position, summarise your key points,
   and reinforce your final judgment.

Remember the requirements: critically respond to ONE takeaway, support your
claims with at least 5 credible secondary sources, and cite everything using
APA 7th edition.
