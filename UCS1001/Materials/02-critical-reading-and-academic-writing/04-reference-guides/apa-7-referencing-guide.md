# APA 7 Referencing Guide

A compact guide to APA (American Psychological Association) 7th edition
referencing. APA is the referencing style used for this module's assignments.
Referencing has two parts: in-text citations and a reference list at the end of
your assignment.

## What is referencing?

Referencing is acknowledging the source(s) of the information, ideas, words,
and images you have used in your assignment. You use referencing to:

- distinguish between your ideas and words and those that belong to other
  people;
- support what you are writing by referring to evidence;
- enable readers to investigate ideas they find interesting or useful;
- show your tutor exactly which sources you have read;
- avoid plagiarism.

## In-text citations

An in-text citation appears in the body of your assignment and can be either a
paraphrase or a quotation.

### Paraphrasing

Paraphrasing is putting in your own words what the author has written. There
are two ways you can reference:

- the author's name incorporated in your text, or
- the author's name and the year of publication included in brackets.

Examples:

> In her book, van der Ham (2016) stresses the fundamental importance of
> education to society.

OR

> Central to modern society is a belief in the importance of education (van der
> Ham, 2016).

### Quoting

Quoting is copying the author's exact words. When quoting, there are two ways
you can reference:

- the author's name incorporated in your text, or
- the author's name included in brackets, together with the year of
  publication and the page number.

Examples:

> "We all know that educational activity is central to society" (van der Ham,
> 2016, p. 1).

OR

> Van der Ham (2016) claims that "We all know that educational activity is
> central to society" (p. 1).

### Three in-text citation patterns

1. Surname + (year) + reporting verb + paraphrased text:
   "Moisse (2010) states that children conceived via assisted reproductive
   technology are more likely to be plagued with obesity, hypertension and Type
   2 diabetes."
2. Preposition + surname + (year) + paraphrased text:
   "According to Moisse (2010), children conceived via assisted reproductive
   technology are more likely to be plagued with obesity, hypertension and Type
   2 diabetes."
3. Paraphrased text + (surname, year):
   "Children conceived via assisted reproductive technology are more likely to
   be plagued with obesity, hypertension and Type 2 diabetes later in life
   (Moisse, 2010)."

## The reference list

- The reference list should be on a separate page at the end of your assignment
  with the title "References" centred at the top, in bold.
- All authors' names begin with the surname(s), followed by their initials. If
  there is no author, place the title in the author position.
- The list is to be in alphabetical order.
- Use hanging indents to format your references.

### Example of a reference list

> References
>
> Barkway, D., & O'Kane, D. (2020). *Psychology: Introduction for health
> professionals*. Elsevier.
>
> Hunt, E. (2019, Spring). Dig your heels in. *Fashion Quarterly, 45*.
>
> McElrea, F., & Thompson, D. (2019, November). Our traditional criminal
> justice system. *The New Zealand Law Journal*, 378-383.

## Authors

All authors' names begin with the surname(s), followed by their initials. If
there is no author, place the title in the author position.

**One author:**

> Diclemente, C. C. (2018). *Addiction and change: How addictions develop and
> addicted people recover* (2nd ed.). The Guildford Press.

**Two authors:**

> McElrea, F., & Thompson, D. (2019, November). Our traditional criminal
> justice system. *The New Zealand Law Journal*, 378-383.

**3 to 20 authors (list all authors):**

> Lamastra, L., Balderacchi, M., Di Guardo, A., Monchiero, M., & Trevisan, M.
> (2016). A novel fuzzy expert system to assess the sustainability of the
> viticulture at the wine-estate scale. *Science of the Total Environment*,
> 572, 724-733. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2016.07.043

**No author (place the title in the author position):**

> New Zealand's 'most instagrammed' spot overrun by illegally parked cars.
> (2019, November 21). *New Zealand Herald*.
> https://www.nzherald.co.nz/travel/news/article.cfm?c_id=7&objectid=12287034

## Books

**Book without DOI:**

> Author(s), A. A. (Year). *Book title in italics*. Publisher.

> Barkway, D., & O'Kane, D. (2020). *Psychology: Introduction for health
> professionals*. Elsevier.

**Book with DOI:**

> Author(s), A. A. (Year). *Book title in italics*. Publisher.
> https://doi.org/xxxx

> American Psychological Association. (2020). *Publication manual of the
> American Psychological Association: The official guide to APA style* (7th
> ed.). https://doi.org/10.1037/0000165-000

Note: this example has no publisher because the publisher is the same as the
author.

**Online book:**

> Author(s), A. A. (Year). *Book title in italics*. Publisher. URL

> National Health Committee. (2015). *The introduction of fit for purpose
> omics-based technologies - Think piece*. Ministry of Health.
> https://www.health.govt.nz/publication/introduction-fit-purpose-omics-based-technologies-think-piece

Note: include a DOI if one is given in place of the URL.

**Chapter in an edited book:**

> Chapter Author, A. A. (Year). Chapter title. In E. E. Editor & F. F. Editor
> (Eds.), *Book title in italics* (pp. xx-xx). Publisher. DOI

> Chand, N. (2019). Standardized turmeric and curcumin. In R. C. Gupta, A.
> Srivastava, & R. Lall (Eds.), *Nutraceuticals in veterinary medicine* (pp.
> 3-24). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-04624-8

## Journal articles

**Journal article - print:**

> Author, A. A. (Year). Article title. *Journal Title in italics, Volume*(Issue),
> pages.

> Foxall, D. (2013). Barriers in education of indigenous nursing students: A
> literature review. *Nursing Praxis in New Zealand, 29*(3), 31-37.

Note: include the DOI after the page numbers if one is given.

**Journal article - online without DOI:**

> Author, A. A. (Year). Article title. *Journal Title in italics, Volume*(Issue),
> pages. URL

> Stanton, R. (2019). Dyslexia and oral skills: A student's journey. *ATLAANZ
> Journal, 4*(1), 55-70.
> https://journal.atlaanz.org/index.php/ATLAANZ/article/view/63/112

**Journal article - online with DOI:**

> Author(s), A. A. (Year). Article title. *Journal Title in italics, Volume*(Issue),
> pages. https://doi.org/xxxx

> Issel, L. M., Bekemeier, B., & Kneipp, S. (2012). A public health nurse
> research agenda. *Public Health Nursing, 29*(4), 330-342.
> https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1525-1446.2011.00989.x

## Webpages

**Webpage:**

> Author, A. A. (Year). Web page title. *Website name*. URL

> Cuncic, A. (2019). The Mandela effect. *Verywellmind*.
> https://www.verywellmind.com/what-is-the-mandela-effect-4589394

**No date:**

Include (n.d.) in place of the publication date.

> Author, A. A. (n.d.). Web page title. *Website name*. URL

> Kalter, L. (n.d.). Ducks & more: Animals offer flyers emotional support.
> *WebMD*.
> https://www.webmd.com/balance/stress-management/news/20191125/ducks-more-animals-offer-flyers-emotional-support
