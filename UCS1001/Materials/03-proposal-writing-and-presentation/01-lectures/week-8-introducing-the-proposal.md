## Purpose Of The Introduction

The Introduction of a report provides a context or background for your
research. It mainly serves to:

- provide background information about the topic in general;
- establish the significance of your study;
- reveal the objective of your study.

There are many ways to write the Introduction:

- an Introduction with the problem statement and the literature review; or
- an Introduction with the problem statement while the literature review is a
  separate chapter (the approach adopted for this module).

## Components Of The Introduction

The Introduction is built from four components:

1. the background paragraph;
2. the problem statement paragraph;
3. the purpose statement;
4. the scope.

### Background Paragraph

To write the background paragraph, you can use the PEEL paragraph framework.
Recall how PEEL is used to structure a paragraph, and apply it to give the
reader the general context needed to understand the topic.

### Problem Statement Paragraph

The problem statement paragraph covers two things:

- context: what the problem is;
- relevance: why it matters and why it is important.

Illustrative pattern: a problem statement might note that the number of people
taking a vaccination has declined significantly over the years, describe the
seriousness of the disease and the risks involved, give a likely reason for the
decline (for example, the rising cost of vaccination), and then explain the
relevance, such as the likely increase in mortality rates and the strain a
specific unprotected group could place on the healthcare system.

### Purpose Statement

The purpose statement summarizes the goal of the document.

- It should flow directly from the problem statement.
- It should be specific and precise, not broad, general, or vague.
- It should be concise, not more than two sentences.
- It should be clear.
- It should be goal-oriented: state the desired outcome(s).

Typical forms include:

- "The purpose of this proposal is to ..."
- "In this proposal, we will describe / explain / review / etc. the ..."
- "This proposal will discuss the ..."
- "The purpose of this proposal is twofold: to ___ and ___."

Ineffective purpose statements are vague, such as "The purpose of this proposal
is to describe the possible ways to improve the marketing strategies for our
client" or "This proposal will cover some effective marketing strategies."

Effective purpose statements are specific, for example describing two main
limitations of a company's current marketing approach and how a three-phase
advertising campaign will drive higher traffic to the sales site within two
months, or explaining how a set of advertising strategies will maximize sales
for a newly launched product, or recommending a promotional campaign that will
improve a company's visibility by a stated percentage.

#### Problem Statement vs Purpose Statement

| Problem statement | Purpose statement |
|---|---|
| Presents the position you intend to argue for | Indicates what the paper aims to achieve |
| Highlights the current problem or undesirable condition | May include solutions |

Example of a purpose statement: "The main objective of this promotional plan is
to encourage more people to turn up for vaccination. The ultimate goal is to
reduce the cost of vaccination, thereby making vaccination more accessible to
all segments of the population."

### Scope

The scope refers to what will be discussed in the proposal. It is reasonable to
also alert your reader to what will not be discussed.

What to include in the scope:

- a general description of what will be covered in the body of the project;
- an overview of the needs and desired outcomes;
- an overview of the limitations, to give a realistic understanding of the
  proposed strategy or strategies.

Useful questions for drafting the scope:

- What are the boundaries and limitations of this proposal?
- What specific tasks and activities will be undertaken as part of this
  project?
- What is the timeline for achieving the proposal's objectives?
- What resources (for example, personnel, budget, technology) will be required
  to implement this proposal?
- What potential risks could arise, and how can they be managed?
- What are the key deliverables and milestones for this project?

## Using AI Tools Critically

You can engage in a critical dialogue with an AI assistant (for example,
ChatGPT) to get feedback on the scope of your proposal, using the questions
above. Cross-referencing and evaluative judgment are critical skills in the
AI age: check that the responses are accurate, and consider to what extent they
align with what you have learned about scope in this unit. Be ready to share
your perspectives with the class, providing details and examples.
