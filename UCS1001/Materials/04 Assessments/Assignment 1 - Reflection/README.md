# Assignment 1 - Reflection

## Quick summary
Individual reflection writing, not weighted towards the grade. Two parts:
a Pre-Learning Reflection of about 300 words submitted in Week 1, and a
Post-Learning Reflection of about 500 words submitted in Week 13 (AY25/26).
Reflections must move beyond description into critical analysis and cite
sources using the APA 7th edition style.

For AY25/26, the weekly schedule describes both reflections as in-class writing

the LMS dropbox, so confirm the current classroom arrangements with the
lecturer.

## Purpose
Demonstrate the ability to analyse, evaluate, and synthesise key learning
points and to write critical reflection essays. Show how learning experiences
contributed to your learning, connect them to your personal goals, and
question your own biases, stereotypes, preconceptions, and assumptions.
Identify and adopt new modes of thinking as a result.

## Requirements

- Complete the critical reflection e-resource on the LMS before writing the
  pre-learning reflection (mandatory).
- Write critical reflection essays of the stated lengths, organised in
  unified, coherent, and cohesive paragraphs.
- Move beyond simple description: analyse how the experience contributed to
  your learning and connect it to your personal goals.
- Cite sources using the APA 7th edition citation style.
- The prompts below are suggestions only; you are not required to answer all
  of them. Select what is relevant and organise it into logical paragraphs.

### Pre-Learning Reflection prompts (Week 1)

- What you personally hope to learn through the module.
- Concerns you have about your ability to succeed.
- How skills or abilities from previous experiences could be applied to
  problems or issues in the module.
- Opportunities you can act on to maximise your learning.
- How the experience might relate to your career path.

### Post-Learning Reflection prompts (Week 13)

- Describe the UCS1001 learning experience.
- How the experience enabled you to apply or transfer learning to a new
  setting, circumstance, or another module in your degree programme.
- How the experience increased your curiosity or awareness about new content
  or issues.
- How you see yourself now compared with the beginning of the experience.
- Which aspect of the experience contributed most to your learning, and why.
- What new insights you developed regarding your career plans.
- How you would describe the engaged learning experience to a potential
  employer, synthesising the knowledge and skills gained.
- Whether you feel you will stay connected with the experience, and why.

See the [Post-Learning Reflection guide](../Post-Learning%20Reflection/README.md) for the
additional distributed prompts for this component.

## Rubric criteria (plain language)

Weights: Content and Organization 75%, Language 25%. Levels: Excellent, Good,
Acceptable, Minimally adequate, Fail.

Content and organization:

- Clear and significant connections to the prompt, with relevant content and
  supporting details and examples.
- A clear focus, with each assertion supported; depth and breadth of detail;
  logical development of ideas throughout each section.
- A complete and fair critical analysis of the learning experience that
  clearly articulates the value of the learning to yourself and/or others.
- Clear and significant integrated connections between the learning
  experience and content from other courses, past learning, life experience,
  and/or personal goals.

Language:

- Clear awareness of audience and purpose, with an appropriate style and tone.
- A significant level of precision, accuracy, and fluency.
- Correct syntax, grammar, vocabulary, and spelling.
- Accurate language for citing sources and correct APA 7th edition usage
  where applicable.

## Preparation order

1. Complete the critical reflection e-resource on the LMS (before Week 1).
2. For the pre-learning reflection, review your goals, concerns, prior
   experiences, and career aims; draft in unified paragraphs.
3. Use the rubric as a self-check: is the analysis critical rather than just
   descriptive, and is the value of the learning made explicit?
4. During the trimester, keep notes on experiences, materials, and feedback
   that shaped your learning, to feed the post-learning reflection.
5. For the post-learning reflection, revisit your module goals, compare your
   starting and ending positions, and reflect on transfer, curiosity, career
   insights, and engagement.
