# Week 6: Technical Proposal Preparation

## Overview

Work through these group tasks in order. Each task builds on the previous one
and uses the MIRO board. By the end of the session your team should have a
strong problem statement, a picture of the current solutions, and relevant
objectives.

## Activity 1: Why The Problem Statement Matters (group discussion)

Discuss the following with your group:

1. Why is the problem statement important?
2. What is generally included in the problem statement?
3. What do we use as sources of information for writing a problem statement?

## Activity 2: Crafting The Problem Statement (group task)

Go to your MIRO board and add a branch to your topic. In this branch, identify
a problem statement for your proposal. You can use any of the four methods
(gap analysis, SWOT analysis, root-cause analysis, or the McKinsey 7S
framework) to help you create a strong problem statement.

By the end of this task, your group should have a good problem statement in the
form of a paragraph.

## Activity 3: Current Solutions (group task)

Go to your MIRO board and brainstorm about the current solutions. These can
come from:

1. your own experience and observation;
2. a quick web search;
3. a systematic literature search (needed when you write your proposal; this
   will be covered in a later session).

## Activity 4: Crafting Objectives (group task)

Go to your MIRO board and consider your problem statement carefully, then craft
relevant objectives.

Remember that objectives must align with the statement of the problem: state
them clearly and specifically, and identify what will be measured to ascertain
the effectiveness of the proposed solution.

## After-Class Homework

Work with your group on the MIRO board. Think about:

- What are you proposing in your project? What is the novelty or appeal of the
  approach recommended?
- The significance of your idea in terms of outcome. How would this be
  attractive and beneficial?
