# Proposal Evaluation Worksheet

## Purpose

Use the Paul-Elder framework to deconstruct your proposal and evaluate the
thinking and reasoning presented in it. Apply intellectual standards to the
elements of thought, focusing on the Introduction and Literature Review
sections.

## Elements Of Thought And Suggested Criteria

For each element of thought, apply the suggested criteria.

### 1. Purpose

What is the author's purpose for writing the proposal? Is the purpose clearly
stated or clearly implied? Is it justifiable? Is it significant? Does it
highlight the urgency of the issue or the severity of the problem?

### 2. Question At Issue / Problem

Identify the key question which the written piece answers. Is the question at
issue well stated? Is it clear and unbiased? Does the expression of the
question do justice to the complexity of the matter at issue? Are the question
and the purpose directly relevant to each other?

### 3. Information

Does the writer cite relevant evidence, experiences, and/or information
essential to the issue? Is the information accurate? Does the writer address
the complexities of the issue? Does the evidence sufficiently support the
conclusions?

### 4. Inferences And Interpretations

Does the writer develop a line of reasoning explaining well how the main
conclusions are arrived at? Are the inferences relevant to the issue or
problem? Do the inferences and conclusions made by the author clearly follow
from the information relevant to the issue, or does the author jump to
unjustifiable conclusions? Do the inferences show depth and breadth?

### 5. Concepts

Does the writer clarify key concepts when necessary? Are the concepts
accurately used?

### 6. Assumptions

Does the writer show a sensitivity to what is being taken for granted or
assumed? Does the writer use questionable assumptions without addressing the
problems which might be inherent in those assumptions?

### 7. Implications And Consequences

Does the writer show sensitivity to the implications and consequences of the
position being taken? Are the implications relevant to the question at issue?
Do the implications and consequences show breadth and depth?

### 8. Points Of View

Does the writer show a sensitivity to alternative relevant points of view or
lines of reasoning? Does the writer consider and respond to objections framed
from other relevant points of view?
