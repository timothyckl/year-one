# Presentation Design and Delivery Guide

## Assignment Overview

The team presentation is delivered in-class. It pitches the proposal to the
same stakeholders for whom it was written. The aim is to persuade the audience
of the merits of the proposed solution and to emphasize its clear benefits for
their needs, rather than simply to explain the proposal.

| Component | Weight | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Individual effectiveness | 80% | Content, verbal skills, non-verbal skills |
| Team performance | 20% | Team dynamics, visual aids |

Assignment logistics:

- aim for about 3 minutes of speaking time per member;
- keep the complete presentation, including Q&A, within 20 minutes;
- prepare every team member to participate actively in the Q&A; and
- submit the slides as one PDF through one team member on the presentation day.

## Preparation Checklist

- Sketch ideas and objectives on paper first (turn off the computer).
- Prepare with the end in mind: know the purpose, the expectations, and the key
  takeaways.
- Know your audience as much as possible.
- Keep it simple: identify the core takeaway in a sentence or two.
- Ask "so what?" and cut content that does not pass the test.
- Prepare with the 10-minute rule in mind: change the pace to hold attention.
- Develop a simple structure (a problem/solution structure works well).
- Use stories and examples to illustrate and support your points.
- Prepare a proper handout that is not just a printout of your slides.
- Work on your opening and ending; make the call to action explicit.
- Have a backup plan for technology failures.

## Design Checklist

- Keep it simple: plenty of empty (white or negative) space.
- Make it visual: pictures are remembered better than words.
- Do not create "slideuments": slides are slides, documents are documents.
- Aim for a high signal-to-noise ratio.
- Design for the back of the room.
- Make images large, often fullscreen.
- Use the principle of contrast to highlight and clarify.
- Use the rule of thirds for balance and interest.
- Use clear charts and graphs: restrain, reduce, emphasize.
- Choose your fonts well: sans serif, large enough to read in an instant.
- Use short video clips for illustration and as a change of pace.
- Use colours with purpose: cool colours for backgrounds, warm colours for
  foreground text.
- Limit animation and transition effects.
- Spend time in the slide sorter to check the logical flow.

## Delivery Checklist

- Start strong with an attention-grabbing opening.
- Show your passion; enthusiasm is contagious.
- Keep it short; finish earlier than expected.
- Move away from the lectern to connect with the audience.
- Use a remote-control device.
- Remember the "B" key to blank the screen and refocus attention.
- Make good eye contact with individuals and smile.
- Keep the lights on; the audience should look at you, not the screen.
- Be courteous, gracious, and professional at all times.
- Dress the part: generally a little more formal than the audience.
- Manage nervousness by knowing the material and rehearsing.
- Meet the audience before the talk.

## Presentation Structure

An effective introduction:

- gains attention and interest and sets the tone;
- introduces the topic clearly;
- relates the topic to the audience;
- establishes the speaker's credibility;
- announces the specific purpose statement;
- previews the body of the speech.

Attention-getting openings include a reference to the subject or occasion, a
personal reference or narrative, a rhetorical question, a startling statement,
humor, a quotation, or a statistic.

An effective conclusion:

- reviews the main points;
- reminds the audience of the importance or significance of what was said;
- asks the audience to take appropriate action;
- ends with a memorable thought;
- thanks the audience.

## Language And Voice

- Project your voice; articulate clearly; be mindful of your pace.
- Be concise and avoid wordiness: "because" not "due to the fact that"; "if"
  not "in the event that".
- Avoid filler words and casual speech ("kind of like", "you know", "sort of").
- Avoid habit words that add no value: "actually", "basically", "and then",
  "so".
- Use language clearly (familiar, concrete words), correctly (grammatical
  accuracy), and appropriately (to the occasion, audience, and topic).
- Use signposting to help the audience navigate, for example "Next, ...", "In
  contrast, ...", "Now that we've discussed ..., I'd like to talk about ...".

## Handling Questions

- Ask for clarification if you do not understand the question.
- Politely avoid answering an irrelevant question.
- Admit when you do not know the answer, and offer to find out.
- Rephrase aggressive questions (for example, "If I understand you correctly,
  you want to know if ...").
- Rephrase negative questions (for example, "I see. So what you're asking is:
  ...").

## Team Preparation And Rehearsal

Agree with your group on:

- the target audience;
- the purpose of the presentation;
- the relevant primary and secondary information;
- how the audience will most effectively be convinced;
- the key takeaways;
- what each member will present;
- how to handle questions you cannot answer;
- how every member will participate in the Q&A.

Rehearse together, allowing about 3 minutes per member and no more than 20
minutes in total including Q&A. Consider the use of visuals, audience engagement
strategies, possible questions and responses, and ways of handling difficult
members of the audience. Include a References slide as the team's final slide.
Submit the slides as one PDF through one team member on the presentation day.
