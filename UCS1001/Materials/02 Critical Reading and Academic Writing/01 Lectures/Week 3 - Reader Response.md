# Week 3: Reader Response

## Overview

A reader response is an evaluative analysis of a discipline-specific article.
This unit covers reading and annotating the text, applying critical thinking
principles from the Paul-Elder framework, and structuring the response essay.

## What you will be able to do

- Read a discipline-specific text and annotate it.
- Respond to one major idea with your own substantiated views.
- Employ the Paul-Elder critical thinking framework while reading and writing.

## Reading critically, annotating, and writing

Before you can understand something well enough to explain it to others, you
have to identify the key points and how they should be organised and explained.
The same is true of any document, article, or chapter you want to read and
explain.

Annotating a text is one way of sorting out its key points and identifying its
organisational structure. Depending on the assignment, you may be asked to
reflect, summarise, critique, evaluate, or analyse the source.

### Contents of an annotation

An annotation may contain all or part of the following elements, depending on
the word limit and the content of the sources you are examining:

- Provide the full citation.
- Indicate the background of the author(s).
- Indicate the content or scope of the text.
- Outline the main argument.
- Identify the research methods, if applicable.
- Identify any conclusions made by the author(s).
- Present your view of the text and your supporting arguments/evidence.
- State the strengths and limitations of the text.
- Discuss the reliability of the text.

## Structure of the reader response essay

- **Introduction:** Name the author(s) and article; briefly describe what the
  text is about; end with your thesis/position statement.
- **Body:** Explain your analysis. Support your claims with evidence from the
  text (specific examples) and information from credible sources. You might
  include what you learned from the text.
- **Conclusion:** Summarise your main supporting ideas to remind the reader of
  your thesis/position. Give a sense of completeness and closure; do not
  introduce a new idea at this point.

## The Paul-Elder critical thinking framework

Use the framework's ten standards of thinking and its elements of thought when
reading and writing.

### Ten standards of thinking

clarity, accuracy, relevance, logic, breadth, precision, significance,
completeness, fairness, and depth.

### Elements of thought

- Purpose (of the reader response).
- Point of view (the student writer's thesis/main claim).
- Assumptions of the student writer's thinking.
- Implications and consequences (of agreeing with the opinion).
- Data, facts, and experiences (evidence offered).
- Concepts and theories (what needs to be understood).
- Questions, issues, and problems (brought forward in the article).

## Assignment 2: the reader response task

- **Type:** critical analysis (reader response).
- **Task:** critically respond to ONE takeaway from the text.
- **Learning outcomes:**
  - Express understanding of a discipline-specific text through critical
    analysis.
  - Apply ethical principles of summarising, paraphrasing, and accurate
    referencing using field-specific conventions.
  - Demonstrate proficiency in sentence structure, paragraphing, and text flow
    in writing.

## Process for the assignment

1. Read the given article carefully, remembering the strategies for critical
   reading:
   - Annotate as you read.
   - Note down the key ideas expressed in the article.
   - Critically evaluate the key ideas:
     - Are the ideas relevant or valid?
     - What evidence does the writer use to support them? Is it relevant,
       credible, acceptable?
     - What assumptions or biases does the writer seem to show?
2. Write up your critical evaluation of the text.

When you critically analyse an idea, you need to do some research on it:

- What do other writers say about it?
- What similar and opposing views are there?

## Source requirements

Use reliable secondary or published sources to support your claims. Refer to at
least 5 credible secondary sources relevant to your topic. Cite all sources
using the APA 7th edition referencing convention.

## Critical response template

### Introduction

1. State the title of the work, the author's name, and the date of publication.
2. Briefly outline the author's thesis or claim and the main ideas of the text.
3. State your own thesis statement or main idea about the text - your
   perspective/critical position.

### Body

- Clearly present the takeaway or key idea you have chosen from the text and
  your perspective on it.
- Support your stance with specific examples, evidence, or citations from the
  text and from credible, recent sources.
- Acknowledge counterarguments fairly, along with their supporting evidence, and
  respond to them thoughtfully.
- Consider multiple core values in your conclusion or solution and balance them
  fairly as the task requires:
  - Ethical (principles and responsibilities),
  - Social (impact on people and communities),
  - Environmental (effects on the planet),
  - Economic (viability and sustainability of the idea).
- Present mitigation strategies for the potential short- and long-term
  limitations or negative impacts of your ideas or solution.
- Ensure that your conclusion or solution is fair, valid, and practical -
  appropriate to the context.
- Briefly discuss what changes or adjustments may be necessary to your proposed
  idea or solution in response to new developments, disruptions, or changes in
  the world (e.g. technology, climate, economy, politics, social values).

### Conclusion

1. Restate your thesis or position using different words.
2. Summarise your key points and insights clearly and concisely.
3. Reinforce your final judgment about the text and your proposed ideas,
   highlighting their relevance and adaptability to future challenges.
