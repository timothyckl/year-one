# UCS1001 Critical Thinking and Reflection

This block distils the first two weeks of UCS1001: an introduction to critical
thinking and communication, the Paul-Elder framework for critical thinking,
systems thinking (interconnection, big picture, feedback loops), and the Week 2
academic and reflective writing skills. It is designed for independent revision
before tutorials and assessments.

## Learning Focus

By working through the material you develop the ability to:

- recognise and explain why communication and critical thinking matter;
- apply the Paul-Elder framework (elements of thought, intellectual standards,
  intellectual traits) to analyse and evaluate arguments;
- consider interconnection, the big picture, and feedback loops when analysing,
  evaluating, and making decisions;
- write unified, coherent, and cohesive paragraphs with effective topic and
  concluding sentences;
- edit and proofread for language, tone, and style;
- use hedges, boosters, and reporting verbs to convey stance;
- reflect on experience in a structured way, moving from description to
  critical reflection.

## Module Learning Outcomes

The module as a whole develops these abilities:

- understand and apply the rhetorical structure of technical and academic
  communication for university writing assignments and presentations;
- apply critical thinking to read content-specific texts, select key points,
  and summarise salient information to support claims;
- apply critical thinking to combine and synthesise information from multiple
  sources to support claims;
- demonstrate good knowledge of sentence structure, paragraphing, and text
  flow when writing;
- apply ethical principles of direct quoting, summarising, paraphrasing, and
  accurate referencing to avoid plagiarising;
- apply critical thinking to produce a report or proposal for a specified
  audience;
- apply critical thinking to deliver an oral presentation to a specified
  audience;
- engage a specified audience through the chosen mode of writing and/or
  speaking.

## Class Participation And Module Expectations

- Prepare before each tutorial: access the course materials and complete the
  assigned tasks on time.
- Participate actively: speak in an informed manner and listen actively to your
  lecturer and classmates.
- Arriving late may be treated as an absence, and classes end 10 minutes before
  the hour.
- Unexcused absences (two consecutive, or three or more in total) may lower the
  participation grade.
- Late submissions receive a deduction of marks; make-up work requires prior
  notice with written confirmation of a legitimate reason.

## Academic Integrity

- Avoid plagiarism, collusion, and other unethical text-borrowing practices.
- Apply ethical practices for direct quoting, summarising, paraphrasing, and
  accurate referencing.
- Use consistent in-text and end-of-text citations, and list sources under a
  "References" heading.

## Grades And Standing

- Repeating a module that was passed with a "D" or "D+" caps the maximum GPA
  for the repeat attempt at 2.0.
- The minimum cumulative grade point average (CGPA) for acceptable standing in

## Using AI Tools Responsibly

- Use AI tools to understand concepts, gather feedback, or improve ideas, but
  treat their output critically.
- Validate the quality and accuracy of any content, examples, and references
  they provide.
- Missing references are treated as academic dishonesty; false information or
  non-existent references may lead to immediate failure.
- Work suspected of being copied from an AI response may trigger an
  investigation and an oral defence.
- Review the learning materials first so you can engage with AI responses
  mindfully rather than accepting them at face value.
