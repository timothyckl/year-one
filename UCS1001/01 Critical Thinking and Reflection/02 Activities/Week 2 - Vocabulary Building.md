# Week 2 - Vocabulary Building

## Overview

This activity practises the vocabulary and phrasing used in reflective writing.
The vocabulary is structured according to a three-part analysis of a reflection:
**description**, **interpretation**, and **outcome**. Using these words and
phrases will not by itself make you a good reflective writer; they support a
clear structure and precise expression.

## Part 1: Description (The Short Bit)

No specific vocabulary is needed for the descriptive part, because the range of
events, ideas, and objects you may need to describe is too great. Follow two
simple rules instead:

- When describing an **idea**, **theory**, or **model**, use the **present
  tense**. Example: "Social interdependence theory recognises that..." (not
  "recognised").
- When describing **events**, use the **past tense**.

## Part 2: Interpretation (Probably The Most Important Bit)

Interpretation is where you make sense of what happened. Use these stems to
signal the significance you attach to an experience.

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Signalling significance | For me, the most meaningful / significant / important / relevant / useful aspect(s), element(s), experience(s), issue(s), or idea(s) was (were)... |
| Locating the learning | My learning arose from..., happened when..., resulted from... |
| Placing the experience in time | Previously, At the time, At first, Initially, Subsequently, Later, Alternatively, Equally |
| Naming your reaction | I thought (did not think)..., felt (did not feel)..., knew (did not know)..., noticed (did not notice)..., questioned (did not question)..., realised (did not realise)... |
| Explaining the cause | This might be / is perhaps / could be because of..., due to..., explained by..., related to... |
| Comparing and concluding | This is similar to... / is unlike..., because...; This reveals..., demonstrates... |

## Part 3: Outcome

The outcome section states what changed and what you will do next. Use these
stems to move from learning to action.

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Opening the outcome | Having read..., experienced..., applied..., discussed..., analysed..., learned...; Additionally, Furthermore |
| Naming the change | I now feel..., think..., realise..., wonder..., question..., know...; I have learned that... |
| Measuring the change | I have significantly / slightly improved (developed) my skills in..., my understanding of..., my knowledge of..., my ability to... |
| Acknowledging what is missing | However, I have not sufficiently developed / improved...; Because I did not..., have not yet..., am not yet certain about..., am not yet confident about..., do not yet know..., do not yet understand... |
| Stating the value | This knowledge / understanding / skill is, could be, will be essential / important / useful to me as a learner (because...), to me as a practitioner (because...) |
| Connecting to the future | This means that..., This makes me feel...; As a next step, I need to...; I will now need to... |

## Practice Tasks

### Task 1: Choose an experience

Pick a recent experience worth reflecting on. Examples: joining a student club,
travelling alone, learning a new software tool, or trying a new sport.

### Task 2: Write the description

Write two or three sentences describing what you did and why. Check your tense:
past tense for the event, present tense for any theory or model you refer to.

### Task 3: Write the interpretation

Write two to three sentences using the interpretation stems. For each sentence,
name the reaction, the cause, or the comparison you are making.

### Task 4: Write the outcome

Write two to three sentences using the outcome stems. Include at least one
sentence about what you have learned and one about your next step.

### Task 5: Peer check

Swap your reflection with a partner and check:

- [ ] The description uses the correct tense.
- [ ] The interpretation goes beyond describing to explain significance.
- [ ] The outcome states what changed and a concrete next step.
- [ ] The vocabulary is varied and precise rather than repeated.
