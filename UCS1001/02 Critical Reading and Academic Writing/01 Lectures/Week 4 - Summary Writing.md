# Week 4: Summary Writing

## Overview

We tend to summarise many things in our lives, both when we speak and when we
write - describing a movie plot, explaining the latest restrictions, or
condensing a reading. A summary is a concise description of the most important
information in a text.

## Why is summary writing important?

Academic writing often includes summaries of:

- relevant information from sources;
- scholars' opinions and arguments;
- theories and concepts.

## Purpose of a summary

A summary provides a concise description of the most important information in a
text:

- the main idea of the text;
- the main points that support the idea.

It also shows how well you have understood a text.

## Steps for writing a summary

1. Read the text closely, critically, and repeatedly.
2. Annotate the text accurately.
3. Identify the main ideas and key examples.
4. Organise the relevant ideas in a logical manner (e.g. cause-effect,
   problem-solution).
5. Connect the ideas with appropriate linking words/connectors.
6. Use your own words to express the relevant ideas (i.e. paraphrase).

## Rules for summarising

1. **Be objective.** Write factual statements; avoid including your own
   opinions.
2. **Be concise.** Avoid long sentences; use words efficiently but don't change
   the writer's meaning.
3. **Paraphrase.** Use linking words/connectors; use synonyms; use different
   sentence structures (e.g. from active to passive, using verbs instead of
   nouns).

## Worked example: identifying reporting verbs

A summary paragraph of an article on climate change and engineers:

> In the article, "Why the climate fight needs engineers," Shah (2015) has
> stated that engineers are needed to achieve the goal of "zero-waste nation"
> established by the Sustainable Singapore Blueprint (SSB). The author even
> quotes Kuttan, director and country manager of the Clean Technology Centre,
> who highlighted that Singapore has one of the world's "highest carbon
> emissions per capita". The author implies that this is adversely causing
> severe climate change, such as "high temperature and droughts". He also
> expresses a belief that engineers equipped with the fundamental knowledge of
> sustainable energy are needed to counter the current concern. He further
> suggests that in the effort to support this demand, Singapore has initiated a
> scheme to groom all levels of engineers, so as to address challenges caused
> by climate change.

Source: Shah, V. (2015, June 8). Why the climate fight needs engineers.
*Eco-Business*.

**Reporting verbs used:** stated, quotes, highlighted, implies, expresses a
belief, suggests.

Note how the summary uses reporting verbs to attribute each point to the
original author, keeps the language objective and concise, and paraphrases the
source's ideas.

## Summarising vs synthesising

| Summarising | Synthesising |
|---|---|
| A basic technique | An advanced technique |
| Presents only the key points from one source (a chapter, a book, an article, etc.) | Combines the main ideas and key details from different sources |
| Gives an overview of a single text | Provides insight into a specific topic by pulling together perspectives from different writers |

- You will need to summarise the information from each source when you combine
  main ideas as part of a synthesis.
- When you synthesise, you summarise the perspectives of different authors on
  the same topic. You can extend it to include your own perspective or
  argument as well.

## Recap

- Summary writing is an important skill at university.
- It allows you to present an overview of a text in a concise manner.
- It shows how much you understand a text.
- When you summarise you must: be objective, be concise, and paraphrase the
  sentences.
- You usually write a summary of ONE text; you combine information from
  different texts to create a synthesised paragraph (for a report or essay).
